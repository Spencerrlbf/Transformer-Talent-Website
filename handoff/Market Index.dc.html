What the data says

SF pays for infra.

Infrastructure and backend carries the strongest bands in San Francisco — median $192–252k with outliers to $600k. If you’re hiring infra in SF on a $180k ceiling, the market will outbid you weekly.

FDE is the arbitrage.

Forward-deployed bands start lower ($140–180k median in SF) but top out at $300–400k for senior operators — the widest spread of any family. Titles are unstandardized; great FDEs are priced by impact, not by band.

For implementation

Every number here is the real data/market-index.json — twelve rows, four families × SF / New York / Other-Remote, totalling 123 searches. Guide pages come from FAMILY_PAGES in lib/market.ts (slug, family, title, keyword, intro, read) with bands via bandsFor(family); both pages are static, so no fetching or loading states exist. Keep the intro and read copy verbatim — they are SEO landing pages targeting specific queries.

SalaryChart stays a server-rendered inline SVG with no chart library: a horizontal range bar per family × city, x domain clamped to $100k–$320k, gridlines every $50k, and a <title> per bar for the native tooltip (“family — city: median band $Xk–$Yk (from N searches)”). It keeps its compact prop for tighter rows. Only two changes: the palette moves to this system’s ink-on-paper values with SF #5B4BFF and NYC #C4621B (hue-distinct, so it survives colour-blind viewing and greyscale printing), and the chart is wrapped in a horizontal scroller with a 640px floor rather than squeezing on narrow screens.

Added, and worth a decision: the four headline stats above the chart (searches, families, strongest band, top of market) are derived, not new data — reduce over the same JSON. The index table gains a market dot matching the chart legend so the two read as one system, and the guide list shows each family’s search count so the sample size is visible before the click.
